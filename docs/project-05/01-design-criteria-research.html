Design Criteria and Background Research

Source document: IGB300_GDD.docx — the Design Criteria and Background Research sections, written by Yusei Nakajima

Design Criteria (Yusei)

Based on the brief and background research, the following design criteria have been established to guide the development of the game concept:

Core Gameplay

  • The game must be built around clear and simple core mechanics that can be understood within the first minute of play.

Player experience

  • Games need to provide a sense of competence through visible progress.
  • The game should maintain player engagement through satisfying feedback and clear responses to player action.
  • The system should be intuitive and easy to understand for new players.

Progression system and Rewards

  • A game requires a clear progression system to encourage continued play.
  • In order to maintain player motivation, rewards must be offered frequently.

Feasibility

  • The design must be achievable within the skill constraints of an 8-person student team.

Background Research (Yusei)

This project brief focuses on video game entrepreneurship, so our background research considered not only the creative aspects of game design, but also market, player experience, and business relevant to developing a viable game concept. The brief mentions innovation, audience appeal, and the ability to take a game from concept to market.

According to Armstrong (2025), almost ⅔ of Americans aged 5 to 90 (205.1 million) play video games regularly, 60 percent of adults play weekly, and the average age is 36, and has played for an average 18 years. The report also indicates that the player base is almost evenly split between men and women. These findings show that common target audience criteria such as gender, age or culture are not necessarily effective at capturing certain portions of the market. This is good news for our team's concept, as we aim to target an audience based on their technology access and daily commitment length.

Research specifically on the idle game genre further supports our concept. Idle game players play an average of about 5.3 sessions per day, for about 8 min per session (Kauppinen, 2019). Based on these findings, we adopted a short, repeatable gameplay cycle centered around quick actions. The card drop system further incentivises this brief player engagement.

Regarding player experience, research has mentioned that players are more likely to continue playing when games offer the ability to fulfill psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness (Zhang et al., 2024; Ballou et al., 2024). According to Ballou et al. (2024), player experience and wellbeing are crucial to the fulfillment and non-fulfillment of needs, and Teng et al. (2024) found that design elements that support autonomy and competence have a significant impact on game continuity and actual use. This is directly reflected in our design. Players exercise autonomy by choosing which surprise mechanics to unlock and which upgrades to prioritize, and their abilities are enhanced by token accumulation, milestone achievement, and a card rarity progression system. These systems ensure that even in a game experience influenced by random elements, players' actions always feel meaningful.
