Research Kenney Assets

We used click, hover, and confirmation sounds from Kenney’s SFX pack to set up the basic interaction layer. Since these assets are available under a CC0 license and have a consistent tone, we were able to significantly reduce the cost per sound effect

using ElevenLabs to generate some SFX

Using ElevenLabs’ sound effect generation feature, I created an initial version of sound effects focused on SMs, such as the impact sound of a coin flip. For each prompt, I iterated using concise and specific descriptions until the results matched the atmosphere of the “Juicy Feedback” mood board from Weeks 8–9. I exported the files in MP4 format and saved them to Google Drive. To make them easier to organize, I created a spreadsheet for the SFX files.

Reference Analysis Gamble With Your Friends

I played Gamble With Your Friends to study how its sound effects create a gambling loop and to analyze its achievements. My main observations are as follows:

  • The sound effects that signal the outcome give a sense of having “earned” a win, while making a loss feel “definitive” without coming across as overly harsh.
  • UI sounds are mixed more subtly than mechanical sound effects, creating a moment of suspense as the result is revealed.
  • Variation is key. Multiple takes of the same sound effect are played in random order, preventing boredom even during extended play sessions.

Create Coin SFX

I created the sound effects for the coin mechanics. The animations for the coin’s bounce and landing were designed to be neutral and independent of the outcome,with all results conveyed solely by the victory sound effect that plays immediately after the collision.

Create Dice Roll SFX

I created sound effects for the dice roll. The shake loop is designed to build anticipation, the roll cue represents the movement, and the landing cue returns to a neutral state. The intensity of the stinger (low/medium/high) indicates the payout.

Plinko SFX

I created the “Plinko” SFX. The slot stinger sounds (1/2, 3, 4) use the same hierarchical structure as the other mechanics.

Poker SFX

I created a poker cue set that covers the entire card-dealing process and the moment the cards are revealed. Since the “card dealing” sequence is repeated for each hand, we have prepared multiple variations.

UI SFX

UI SFX for menus, confirmations, and feedback. Most of these are sourced from Kenney's Interface Sounds pack to ensure tonal consistency.

Weeks 2–3

Playtest for SFX – 1.5h

I playtested the Surprise Mechanic and UI SFX, focusing on clarity, volume, repetition, and thematic fit. Results indicated that repeated coin-flip sounds are uncomfortably high-pitched, and button-click and reward sounds are too loud. Overall, sounds lacked necessary excitement; while Crash feedback is strong, Poker and Plinko require more impact. These findings will guide the next SFX iteration.

Add Some Code for SFX Playtest – 1h

I added temporary testing code that allowed important SFX to be triggered and compared more easily in Unity. This helped me repeatedly test sounds without listening to any other BGM and Sound Effect. I used the test setup to compare volume, pitch, repetition, and differences between standard and high-value outcomes.

Target Research for SFX – 0.5h

I researched what types of SFX our target audience prefers in gambling and idle games. I used the findings to guide the game’s audio direction and make the sounds more appealing to our target players. Based on these insightss, quiet clicking sounds will be used for standard actions, while dramatic sound effects are reserved for key moments such as big win or etc.
