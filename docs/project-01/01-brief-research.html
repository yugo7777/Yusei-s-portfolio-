Brief Research

ECO DEFENDERS · IGB200 Group 3.2 · Jul–Nov 2025 · Author: Yusei

IGB200 project brief B research

Purpose:

To teach players the process of planning, designing and building an eco-home through a game.

To understand the meaning of “eco-home” and to increase knowledge and awareness of sustainability.

Introduce the specific roles of construction-related professions (electricians, carpenters, plumbers, etc.).

What is an eco-home?

A home that uses energy and water resources more efficiently than conventional homes and reduces environmental impact.

Passive design (building orientation, design that makes use of natural light and natural ventilation), use of renewable materials, water-saving and energy-saving equipment (solar panels, rainwater tanks, etc.).

Building industry career path:

Electrician: promoting energy-saving lighting and renewable energy solutions.

Carpenter/builder: utilising sustainable materials and reducing construction waste.

Plumber: introducing water-saving technology, energy-efficient plumbing fixtures and rainwater reuse systems.

Target group:

Fifth and sixth graders (10-12 years).

Games are designed to be intuitive and easy to understand, moderately complex and playful.

Reference game examples: Minecraft, The Sims, Serious game

Essential requirements in game design:

Players can freely decide on the floor plan and design.

A system whereby players can choose eco-materials and energy-saving equipment, which are then linked to ratings such as the in-game “energy efficiency meter”.

Explicit introduction of CSQ-proposed professions as in-game characters to guide the construction process.

Visually attractive and easy to operate. Balance between fun and educational.

Organisational Background (CSQ):

A not-for-profit organisation that supports skills development in the building industry.

It promotes building careers and provides training in Queensland.

Promotes understanding of the building industry through job placement for students (e.g. construction managers, electricians, surveyors, labourers, plumbers, carpenters, civil engineers).

Existing games survey (reference and differentiation):

The Sims: allows freedom of house design and decoration. However, focus on sustainability is weak.

Minecraft: resource management, construction elements utilising creativity. However, little eco-design and specific job introduction.

Academic background on sustainability:

Sustainability has been emphasised in recent years, as the construction industry has a significant impact on the environment (Lima, 2021).

Sustainable construction addresses not only initial costs but also long-term maintenance and operating costs (Ayarkwa, 2022).

Sustainable construction (green building) considers environmental protection in the whole process from construction, operation and maintenance to demolition (Kumar, 2024).

Suggested game idea:

Players use their creative freedom to design houses using environmentally friendly materials.

Career characters in the game provide advice and information at each stage.

Completion of the houses is assessed by an energy efficiency score and rewarded accordingly.

A level system and refurbishment scenarios are introduced to learn about sustainability in depth through more challenging designs.

Draft questions (for industry partners):

  • Should it be free-design or clear goal-oriented, with an emphasis on creativity?
  • Is it preferable to include career skills such as plumbing and electrical wiring as mini-games?
  • Should the job description be only an overview or should it go into details such as skills and salary?
  • With regard to game design, should it incorporate specific colours or themes to match the CSQ website?
  • Is a 3D or 2D game preferred?
  • As an educational game, should it include quizzes to measure players' understanding?
